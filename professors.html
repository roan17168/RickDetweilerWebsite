For Professors

"How can I prove that my teaching has a lasting impact on my students' lives?"

You've felt the pressure of a "vocational-first" climate that asks you to justify the humanities, the arts, the sciences in narrow economic terms. The evidence finally gives you what you've needed: empirical proof that the way you teach changes lives.

The Short Answer

Detweiler's research validates what you've always known: the relationship between teacher and student is the heart of education. Faculty who encourage big-picture thinking, who connect with students outside class, who challenge them to ask hard questions — these professors produce graduates who lead lives of consequence. Your work matters, and now there's data to prove it.

"I sought to use what I hoped would be a more persuasive and productive approach... to assess whether there is a statistical relationship between liberal arts content of study and educational context and the long-term life outcomes of graduates."
— Richard A. Detweiler, The Evidence Liberal Arts Needs

The research empirically links specific pedagogical practices to long-term life outcomes. Students who experience frequent class discussion, are pushed to think across disciplines, and engage in inquiry-driven learning are significantly more likely to become civic leaders and report deeper life satisfaction.

The implication: your classroom design choices today shape lives forty years from now.

Mentorship outside the classroom emerged as one of the strongest predictors of student outcomes. The hour you spend in office hours, the lunch you share with a student, the recommendation letter you write — these are not extras. They are the heart of what makes higher education transformative.

The data validates investing in relationships, not just content delivery.

When asked to justify your department's existence, you no longer have to rely on philosophical appeals. You can point to a rigorous empirical study of 1,000 graduates across 50 years that demonstrates:

  • Liberal arts graduates report higher career success and life satisfaction
  • Broad study correlates with civic engagement and community leadership
  • Inquiry-based teaching predicts adaptability in changing economies

The evidence is on your side. Use it.
