Research & Methodology

How the evidence was gathered and analyzed

Detweiler's study draws on structured interviews with 1,000 college graduates across the United States, sampled from a wide range of institutions — large and small, public and private, liberal arts and non-liberal arts.

Participants spanned graduating cohorts from 10, 20, and 40 years prior, allowing the research to trace long-term life outcomes including leadership, altruism, continued learning, cultural involvement, fulfillment, and career success.

The study analyzed three interconnected dimensions of liberal arts education:

  • Content: Amount of humanities study, kinds of course assignments, major
  • Context: Out-of-class interaction with faculty and other students, campus involvement, teaching methods experienced
  • Purpose: Long-term life outcomes such as leadership, altruism, lifelong learning, and fulfillment

The integration of these three dimensions — not any one in isolation — produced the strongest predictive power for adult life outcomes.
