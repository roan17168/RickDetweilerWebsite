Your college scorecard

What it’s for. The scorecard turns your visits, calls and research into one page you can compare. It records what you found out about each college, not what the college says about itself.

When to use it. After you take the quiz, and once you have two to four finalists. Fill it in as you go: after reading a college’s website, after a visit or virtual tour, after talking with a current student.

If you took the Choosing a College quiz, your result is starred below.

How to use it

  1. Write your finalists across the top. Up to four colleges, in columns #1 to #4. (Make more copies if you want to compare more colleges.)
  2. Star your result. Put a star next to the row for your top result from the quiz. If you had two top results, star both. Starred rows count most when you compare.
  3. Check the dealbreakers first. These are pass or fail. If a college fails one, it is probably out, however well it does on the rest.
  4. Gather evidence for each row. Each row tells you where to look and what to ask. You can use any of three kinds of evidence:
    • Look it up. The college’s website, course catalog, student newspaper, events calendar and outcomes page.
    • Ask. A current student is the best source. Admissions staff are useful for facts like requirements and costs.
    • Watch. On a visit, sit in on a class, walk through the student center on a weekday, read the bulletin boards.
  5. Rate each row using the scale below. Leave it as “Don’t know yet” until you have real evidence.
  6. Write down the best example you heard. One sentence per college. Specific stories are what you’ll remember later.
  7. Compare. Look at the dealbreakers first, then your starred rows, then everything else. Count the Strongs, but read the examples too.

The rating scale

The rating scale: what each rating means
RatingWhat it means
StrongA current student gave you a specific, recent example, or you saw it yourself.
MixedThere’s some evidence, or it depends on the major or program, or students disagreed.
WeakStudents couldn’t give an example, or what you found suggests it’s rare.
Don’t know yetYou haven’t found real evidence. A website claim alone stays here.

The form

Write the name of each finalist at the top of its column, #1 to #4, and use the same order in every part.

On the site, the scorecard saves on your device. Use Copy or Email to keep a copy, or use Print to print the page or save it as a PDF.
