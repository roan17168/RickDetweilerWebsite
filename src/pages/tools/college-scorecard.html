Tool

Your college scorecard

What it’s for. Turn your visits, calls, virtual sessions and research into one page you can compare. It records what you found out about each college, not what the college says about itself.

If you took the Choosing a College quiz, your result is starred below.

How to use it

Write your finalists across the top and choose the kind of college for each (large university, small college, community college, other). Add up to four rows of your own at the bottom: anything that matters to you, such as a sport, a program, distance from home, or a place you can practice your faith. Gather evidence for each row and rate: Strong, Mixed, Weak, or Don’t know yet. A claim on the college’s website alone counts as “Don’t know yet.”

The rating scale

The rating scale: what each rating means
RatingWhat it means
StrongA current student gave you a specific, recent example, or you saw it yourself.
MixedThere’s some evidence, or it depends on the major or program, or students disagreed.
WeakStudents couldn’t give an example, or what you found suggests it’s rare.
Don’t know yetYou haven’t found real evidence. A website claim alone stays here.

The form

Write the name of each finalist at the top of its column, #1 to #4, and use the same order in every part.

On the site, the scorecard saves on your device. Use Copy or Email to keep a copy, or use Print to print the page or save it as a PDF.

College Navigator is the US Department of Education’s college search tool (nces.ed.gov/collegenavigator). Every college is required to post a net price calculator.
