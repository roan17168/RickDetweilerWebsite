Policy brief generator

What it’s for. Formulate a formal legislative report request or committee briefing on long-term higher education outcomes for your state.1

The federal version frames the request around national purposes for higher education: the six life outcomes and the college experiences linked to them.

The five measures

Mark whether your state or agency collects each measure, who could collect it, and how high a priority it is (1 is highest).

Earnings and employment ten, twenty and thirty years after graduation

Why it matters. Early advantages by major fade; in most cases, major was unrelated to longer-term success. Outcomes at graduation or in the first few years miss this.

Share of credits taken outside the major

Why it matters. Graduates who took more than half their courses outside the major were 24% more likely to report a higher income; over a long career, 72% more likely (75% for graduates from lower-income families).

Share of students with a mentor

Why it matters. Graduates with a lasting mentor were 26% more likely to be leaders, 28% more likely to volunteer and 34% more likely to report fulfilled lives.

Student engagement survey results, including an authentic learning community (faculty contact outside class, mentoring, campus involvement)

Why it matters. What students do in college matters more than where they enroll; an authentic learning community was linked to all six adult outcomes, and engagement is linked to finishing.

Alumni civic involvement ten, twenty and thirty years out: volunteering, giving, leadership

Why it matters. A direct measure of public return over a lifetime.

Model legislative report request language

“The [agency] shall report to the [committee] by [date], for each public institution: (a) median earnings of graduates ten and twenty years after completion, using state wage records, and thirty years after completion as records allow; (b) results of alumni surveys of the same graduates on leadership, civic involvement and continued learning; (c) the share of graduates who completed more than half of their credits outside their major; and (d) results of a student engagement survey on students’ contact with faculty outside class, alongside the completion and early-earnings data already reported. Existing records and surveys shall be used wherever possible.”

For a hearing or floor statement

“A first job is a floor, not a ceiling. Graduates who studied widely and had professors who knew them earned more, led more and gave more decades later, and the gains were largest for students from families with little money. We should keep holding colleges to the floor, and start measuring the rest.”
