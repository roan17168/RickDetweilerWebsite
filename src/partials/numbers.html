How to read these numbers

A figure such as “24% more likely” compares two groups. For example, if 57% of one group reports a higher income, compared with 46% of the other group, the first group’s rate is 124% of the second group’s (57 divided by 46). Statisticians call this the incident risk ratio, and the increase expressed as a percentage (24%) is called the percent relative effect. All reported effects are statistically significant at the p<.05 level or better; most are significant at the p<.001 level.
